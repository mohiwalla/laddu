Everyone gets a private laddu, with a desktop of its own.

Each person who tags laddu gets their own computer: a private desktop in its own container on your server, with a browser, files that stick around between conversations, and the internet. laddu clicks and types its way through the things no API reaches.

  • Watch from laddu’s Home tab in Slack, take over with one click, and hand it back.
  • Files and the browser stay put between conversations, so it picks up where it left off.
  • Your teammates get their own, and no laddu can get into anyone else’s computer.

Brains, your pick.

Codex, on the ChatGPT plan you sign in with during setup, or OpenCode’s free models. Everyone chooses their own on laddu’s Home tab, along with the model and how hard it thinks.

Go on, pick one. It’s the same choice everyone gets in Slack.

Runs on your server
A systemd service on your own Linux machine. Each request goes to the brain that person picked: Codex on the ChatGPT plan you signed in with, or OpenCode’s free models.
Internet only
Each person’s computer reaches the internet and nothing else: no way into your servers, your network or anyone else’s computer.
Your ChatGPT sign-in stays outside

Sees only what you can

Just you, everyone in the workspace, or specific people. Pick during setup; change it anytime with laddu configure.

One command, then a few friendly questions.

Run this on the Linux server where laddu will live; over SSH from your laptop is fine. It adds Docker and Bun if they’re missing, then walks you through the rest. Running it again repairs or resumes an install.

curl -fsSL https://laddu.wtf/install.sh | sh

Read the script first Server already has Bun and Docker? bun create laddu does the same.

What happens

  1. Asks a few basics. Its name, whether each person gets their own computer, and an optional public web address for watching its desktop. Every question has a default.
  2. Connects to Slack. Paste the command it prints into Slack, approve, and type the code Slack shows back into the terminal. Then pick who can use it.
  3. Signs in to ChatGPT. Open the link it prints on your laptop or phone, sign in, and paste the address your browser lands on back into the terminal. Then pick a model.
  4. Says hi. It starts as a service and DMs you in Slack. The first start takes a few minutes.

What you need

  • A Linux server with systemd (x86_64 or arm64). Run the command there, from any laptop.
  • A Slack workspace where you can add apps.
  • A ChatGPT account.
  • Optional: a public HTTPS address, to watch laddu’s desktop.
